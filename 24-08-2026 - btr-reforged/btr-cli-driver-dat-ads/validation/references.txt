PRIMARY TECHNICAL RESEARCH
Check Point Research, "BTR Reforged: Weaponizing Defender's Remediation Driver
as a Kernel Operation Primitive."
Publication date: 2026-08-20. Accessed: 2026-08-24.
The researchers reverse engineered BTR.sys and reproduced the BTR_CLI PoC. In
Sysmon Event ID 15 they observed the PoC writing feedback to a secondary .dat
ADS on the .sys driver, whereas legitimate MpEngine.dll wrote feedback as a
standalone file under the Windows Defender RebootActions directory. They did
not observe real-world abuse.

EVIDENCE BOUNDARY
The query validates the current BTR_CLI-specific feedback artifact. A match is
not proof that BTR.sys executed malicious operations, that Defender was
disabled, or that a real attacker was present. The universal .sys:changelist
stream is deliberately excluded because Check Point observed it in both
legitimate Defender remediation and the PoC. EventData.TargetFilename must be
confirmed against a raw Event ID 15 sample in the target workspace.
